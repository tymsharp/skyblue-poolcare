Hard Water and Calcium Scale in LA Pools

Why a white crust shows up on the tile line, what calcium hardness level to hold, and when a drain and refill makes sense.

Southern California tap water is hard, meaning it carries a lot of dissolved calcium. Every time your pool tops itself off after a hot week, it adds more. Water evaporates; the calcium stays.

What scale looks like

A white or gray crust along the tile line, rough spots on plaster, and white flakes in the water. Scale also builds inside heaters and on salt cells, where you can't see it but it shortens their life.

The level to hold

Most pool pros aim for calcium hardness between about 200 and 400 parts per million. Scale forms faster when calcium is high and pH and alkalinity drift up at the same time, so the three have to be managed together. That's why we test every week instead of guessing.

Burbank vs Los Angeles water

Burbank homes get water from Burbank Water and Power, not LADWP. The supply is different, so the pool's balance drifts differently. See our Burbank pool service page.

When to drain and refill

When calcium climbs so high that the water won't stay balanced, a partial or full drain and refill resets it. Acid washing removes stains and scale from older plaster at the same time. Drains are best done in cooler months, not in a September heat wave.

Why Pools Turn Green in a San Fernando Valley Heat Wave

Valley heat burns off chlorine fast. Here's why pools flip green in a few days and how weekly service prevents it.

The San Fernando Valley gets some of the hottest days in Los Angeles County. Woodland Hills set the county record at 121°F on September 6, 2020. Pools feel it.

What heat does to your water

  • Sunlight breaks chlorine down. Without stabilizer, most of it can be gone in an afternoon.
  • Warm water grows algae faster.
  • More swimmers add more sunscreen, sweat and debris that use up chlorine.
  • Evaporation means more top-off water, which shifts the balance.

How to keep it clear

  • Hold stabilizer (cyanuric acid) in range so chlorine survives the sun.
  • Test and adjust every week, not every few weeks.
  • Keep the filter clean so it can catch what the chlorine kills.
  • Run the pump long enough on hot weeks to turn the water over.

If it's already green

Our green-to-blue cleanup brings it back with algae treatment, brushing and a filter clean. Then weekly service keeps it there. Serving Woodland Hills, Encino, Northridge, Tarzana and the rest of the Valley.
